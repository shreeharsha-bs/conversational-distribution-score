What do distributions of conversational behaviour tell us about a speech system?

CDS compares measured behaviour with two reference collections. Explore how the results change with the feature, the reference and the unit of comparison.

How to read this result

    Definitions before interpretation

    How the scores work

    A higher CDS means a distribution is closer to the high-success human reference relative to the second anchor. It is not a probability of being human or a universal quality rating.

    CDS = 100 ×distance to second anchordistance to HSC + distance to second anchor
    What is one observation?

    Phone rate, Sylber rate, nPVI and final lengthening are measured per eligible turn. Sylber duration contributes individual segment durations. Interaction features describe turn spans, cross-speaker response gaps and each speaker’s floor-time share. These different observation types form separate distributions.

    Pace and rhythm use 3–30 second speech spans. Rhythm measurements need at least three valid Sylber segments. Interaction measurements use the complete speech timeline.

    How do conversations receive equal weight?

    Each conversation contributes equal total mass to a group distribution. Within a conversation, retained speakers share that mass equally. Turn measurements divide a speaker’s mass among valid turns; Sylber segments divide each turn’s mass among its segments. Response gaps share mass across transitions.

    For example, conversations with 90 and 10 turns each contribute half of the group distribution, rather than 90% and 10%.

    What are the reference collections?

    HSC and MSC are high- and medium-success human conversations from CANDOR. Behaviour-SD is the separate synthetic-dialogue anchor. The behavioural analyses use 50 frozen HSC selections, with 34 reference conversations per selection. The MSC and Behaviour-SD anchors contain 35 and 120 conversations respectively.

    The group plots also show 34 low-success CANDOR conversations (LSC), a separate target collection from the 35 medium-success conversations used as the MSC anchor.

    Held-out HSC plots also change their 34 target conversations across selections. A cloud of 50 reference-split scores describes sensitivity to these selections, not 50 individual conversations.

    What gets averaged?

    The main CDS averages features within pace, rhythm and interaction, then gives those three families equal weight. It contains eight behavioural features. Linguistic CDS is a separate average of NLI and GPT-2 feature scores.

    Distances are exact scalar empirical Wasserstein-2 distances, including the square root. When both component distances are zero, the score is 50 and the case is flagged. A score of 50 otherwise means equal distances; both distances may still be large.

    Why are there several listener comparisons?

    Conversation-pair correlation asks whether a larger score difference accompanies a stronger listener preference. The main comparison uses the same 294 usable pairs from 51 callers. Repeated votes become one proportion per pair, and ties count as half a vote.

    Both orientations of each pair are included to remove arbitrary A/B ordering. They are not extra independent observations. Caller ranges use 2,000 resamples of complete callers within collection protocol, holding listeners and references fixed.

    System-ordering plots instead compare caller/scenario-adjusted conversation scores with a system Bradley–Terry ranking. Four systems give six possible system comparisons. Their population can differ from the matched-pair population; each view states its counts.

    What do conversation BT clouds mean?

    Each point is one rated conversation. Its vertical position is a relative Bradley–Terry log strength, centered within its connected caller/scenario comparison group. Zero is a centering convention, not a humanness threshold.

    The green human controls are Voice Arena partners, not the CANDOR references. These descriptive clouds do not show the matched-pair correlation used in the listener tables. Side-by-side comparisons retain the same available conversations.

    What does the weighting ablation change?

    The ablation changes hierarchical versus equal-observation weights in the system targets, HSC references and second anchors together. It does not remove distance-ratio calibration or change feature-family weights.

    Table values are means and sample standard deviations across 50 overlapping HSC selections. The SD describes reference sensitivity, not a confidence interval. Pooled system scores are different from averages or adjusted estimates of conversation scores.

    How should TTSDS2 and linguistic scores be read?

    The historical TTSDS2 conversation scores use 100 turns from one HSC conversation and the closest official noise reference. The later pooled-system clouds use 50 broader HSC draws. Those pooled results do not establish reference robustness of the historical listener correlations.

    NLI uses the model’s entailment probability for a reply given two preceding chronological turns. GPT-2 measures mean response surprisal in bits, equal to log₂ perplexity. These exploratory features remain separate from the eight-feature composite.

    How are callers and recording lengths sampled?

    For each caller count below 56, the recording-length analysis draws 2,000 caller subsets without replacement within each draw. The same subsets are reused across durations. At 56 callers there is one complete set.

    Ranking recovery compares each estimator with its own full-recording ranking. Correlations use listener preferences about the complete conversations, even when scores use shorter excerpts. Their bands describe variation among caller subsets; undefined correlations are not replaced with zero.
